## 1. The Phenomenon

Three anchor cases discussed:

- **Armenian Genocide (Ottoman Empire):** Armenians were Ottoman subjects -- shared territory, military service, economic integration, substantial shared narrative. The Young Turk faction constructed a counter-narrative (Armenians as Russian fifth column) that overrode shared history, enabling Dominion-force application without normal internal constraints.

- **US (current):** Anyone non-white is being reclassified as "immigrant / enemy within." People rounded up and detained without legal recourse despite clear Constitutional framework. The counter-narrative collapses the distinction between citizens of color and undocumented arrivals -- applied selectively by race.

- **Iran (post-1979):** Shared narrative is Islam itself, but the Sol performs a purity inversion -- only a specific interpretation of Islam is authentic. Western-influenced Iranians are not coded as non-Iranian but as *corrupted* Iranians, which is more threatening in narrative terms than a foreign enemy because they contaminate from within.

## 2. The Core 1G vs 2G Question

**Initial framing:** Is this 1G or 2G?

**Resolution reached:** It is a 1G construct that produces *pseudo-2G dynamics* as an emergent property.

**Why 1G:** The excluded group remains physically, historically, and field-embedded within the same social spacetime. Same territory, same legal framework (formally), overlapping Psi, shared historical inertia. The manifold is one.

**Why it feels like 2G:** The Political Sol unilaterally *declares* the excluded subpopulation to be a separate Mass and applies Dominion-force protocols appropriate for external enemies -- without the constraints that apply to internal subjects. The Sol is manufacturing a 2G interaction condition inside a 1G geometry.

**The key structural paradox:** In genuine 2G, the two Masses have actually separate manifolds. The separation is real and the interaction cost reflects real field distance. In the "enemy within" case, the separation is *imposed*, not real. The Sol is trying to enforce 2G interaction protocols on a population that remains embedded in the 1G field. This fights the underlying geometry continuously.

## 4. Candidate Construct: Manifold Partition

**Provisional name:** Manifold Partition

**Definition (working):** A Sol-imposed boundary drawn *within* a single Mass volume that designates a subpopulation as outside the protective scope of the Configuration, while they remain inside the physical and historical manifold.

**Distinguishing properties (vs ordinary repression):**

1. **Legal standing revocation** -- not just suppression of behavior but removal of the formal inclusion framework. The subpopulation loses standing as subjects, not just as actors. They become deportable, detainable, killable under a different legal logic than internal subjects.

2. **Narrative inversion requirement** -- the Sol must actively construct and maintain a counter-narrative that overrides shared history. This counter-narrative doesn't need to be true; it needs to be politically functional. But it is energetically expensive and structurally fragile -- the underlying Psi field keeps pulling toward reintegration because the actual shared history is real.

3. **Dominion without constraint** -- once the partition is declared, D_dom force can be applied at levels that would otherwise require justification to the broader Mass.

4. **Field tension** -- the imposed partition fights the underlying manifold geometry. The excluded group's actual Psi overlap with the broader population creates constant pressure against the partition boundary. This is why these campaigns always escalate -- the Sol must keep amplifying the exclusion narrative because the field keeps resisting it.

**Emergent instability:** Because the partition is imposed against the field geometry, it is inherently unsustainable at low energy. The Sol must continuously invest in narrative maintenance and coercive enforcement to hold the partition. This drains E_m, raises system-wide Omega_acc, and tends toward either full resolution (the partition collapses and the group is reintegrated) or catastrophic completion (genocide -- the Sol resolves the field tension by physically removing the contested subpopulation from the manifold entirely).

## 5. Open Questions for Next Session

**Primary unresolved question:** Which feature is the *load-bearing* one for formal TSR treatment?

- **Option A: Legal standing revocation** -- the formal removal of Constitutional/institutional inclusion. This is the mechanism that enables Dominion without constraint. Without it, repression is limited by internal legal logic.
- **Option B: Narrative inversion** -- the active counter-narrative construction that overrides shared Psi. This is what makes the broader population complicit (or at least non-resistant). Without it, the partition has no social legitimacy and requires pure coercion.

These may be jointly necessary rather than alternatives -- legal standing revocation enables the act; narrative inversion enables the consent. But which one *initiates* the dynamic and which follows?

**Secondary questions:**
- Does Manifold Partition need to be a named construct, or does it emerge cleanly enough from existing constructs (Sol behavior + Psi manipulation + D_dom escalation) that it doesn't need its own name?
- How does the *intensity* of shared narrative prior to partition affect the energy cost and trajectory? (Armenian case: very high prior integration. Iranian case: shared religious identity but interpreted differently. US case: constitutional framework as shared narrative being overridden.)
- Is there a Phase threshold condition -- a point at which a Manifold Partition attempt becomes self-defeating (the energy cost of maintaining it exceeds system capacity, triggering either collapse or genocide)?
- Connection to Omega_acc: does an imposed partition accelerate Omega_acc accumulation in the targeted subpopulation AND in the broader Mass (through complicity stress, moral injury, dissipation channel closure)?
